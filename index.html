About

Glioblastoma is the most common and most aggressive primary brain tumor in adults, and its prognosis has changed little in two decades. Part of the reason is that the tumor is not only a mass of malignant cells: it recruits and reshapes the immune, vascular and neural tissue around it.

This workshop brings together cancer biologists, neuroscientists, engineers and clinicians to look at glioblastoma as a whole system, from the molecular programs of tumor cells and their microenvironment to imaging, surgery and new therapeutic strategies.

The GENTE project

Glioblastoma progression is favoured by a disrupted balance between excitation and inhibition in cortical circuits, linked to damage to parvalbumin-positive (PV) interneurons. These cells are key to generating cortical oscillations in the gamma band (30–100 Hz).

GENTE tests a non-invasive way to counter tumor progression: visual stimulation that entrains gamma oscillations in the peritumoral cortex (GENUS, Gamma ENtrainment Using Sensory stimulation). The aim is to find out whether gamma-range stimulation can selectively reactivate PV neurons and, through them, limit tumor proliferation. The project combines electrophysiological recordings, optogenetic stimulation and computational modelling. It compares visual stimulation, endogenous gamma oscillations evoked by non-rhythmic visual patterns, and direct stimulation of PV cells at non-gamma frequencies, and explores the role of other interneuron subtypes, such as somatostatin-positive cells.

The consortium brings together the CNR Institute of Neuroscience in Pisa and the Sant'Anna School of Advanced Studies, which have long worked together on cortical circuits in mouse models of glioma. Expected results include the stimulation parameters that best slow tumor growth and a non-invasive neuromodulation protocol with potential clinical application.
